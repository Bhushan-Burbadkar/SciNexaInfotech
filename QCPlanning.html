Industry Challenges

Why QC labs struggle with planning today

Most laboratories still coordinate testing through disconnected trackers, manual calendars, and local decisions. That makes utilization, turnaround performance, and visibility harder than they need to be.

1

Inefficient Resource Utilization

Analyst time and instrument capacity are hard to balance when planning sits in spreadsheets and email threads.

2

Manual Scheduling Delays

Release testing and priority samples get slowed down by reactive scheduling and hidden workload conflicts.

3

Fragmented Test Execution

Disconnected workflows increase handoffs, duplicate coordination, and missed context at the bench.

4

Underused Instruments

Poor campaign planning leads to unnecessary setup changes, idle windows, and lower throughput.

5

Weak Real-Time Visibility

Leaders cannot reliably see pending load, analyst capacity, approaching deadlines, and bottlenecks in one place.

End-to-End Process Flow

How QC Planning & Scheduling works

A structured workflow from sample request through reporting, with built-in traceability at every checkpoint.

1

Test Request & Sample Registration

Capture requests through LIMS, ERP triggers, or manual entry with predefined plans and initial priorities.

2

Intelligent Scheduling & Allocation

Assign work to qualified analysts and available instruments using rules for workload, due dates, and readiness.

3

Campaign Grouping & Batching

Group similar tests by product, method, or turnaround target to minimize setup changes and wasted bench time.

4

Single-Click Test Execution

Launch prepared runs with the right sample list, methods, and execution context already in place.

5

Real-Time Monitoring & Alerts

Track active tests, pending queues, analyst load, and equipment status with escalation for deadline risk.

6

Compliance Reporting

Generate TAT reports, productivity metrics, utilization evidence, and audit-ready history without manual reconstruction.
